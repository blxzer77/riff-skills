# Design document baseline

A useful proposal makes a decision legible to a future reader. Use these sections as
a baseline; preserve an existing project template when one exists.

- Context and problem: current state, users, constraints, and why now.
- Goals and non-goals: what success includes and what is explicitly out.
- Decision drivers: quality attributes and constraints, in priority order.
- Proposed design: boundaries, responsibilities, data/control flow, and lifecycle.
- Alternatives considered: viable options and why each lost.
- Failure and recovery: timeouts, duplicates, partial failure, restart, rollback.
- Security and privacy: trust boundaries, data exposure, permissions, secrets.
- Verification: tests, invariants, migration rehearsal, and measurable success.
- Consequences: benefits, costs, follow-on work, and exit/revisit triggers.

The Google design-doc guidance also emphasizes context, scope, alternatives, and
cross-cutting concerns. Source: Malte Ubl, “Design Docs at Google”,
https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/ (checked 2026-10-30).
